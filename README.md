# VoltRelay Energy – Data Analytics Hackathon Project

## 🚀 Project Overview

This project analyzes the VoltRelay Energy battery-swapping network to understand business performance, identify operational challenges, and provide data-driven business recommendations.

The project was developed as part of the **Gradient Learnings Data Analytics Hackathon – November 2026**.

## 🎯 Problem Statement

To analyze battery-swapping operations across multiple cities and identify opportunities to improve operational efficiency, customer experience, battery utilization, and overall business performance.

## 🛠️ Tools & Technologies

- Python
- Pandas
- SQL
- DuckDB
- Matplotlib
- Google Colab

## 📂 Dataset Description

The project uses multiple datasets related to:

- Swap Events
- Batteries
- Stations
- Riders
- Fleet Partners
- City Daily Context
- Station Hourly Status
- Support Tickets

## 📊 Analysis Performed

### Q1. Monthly Business Performance
Analyzed monthly swap attempts, completed swaps, non-completion rates, revenue, energy contribution, and average queue time.

### Q2. City & Vehicle Performance
Compared two-wheeler and three-wheeler performance across six cities and identified high non-completion and stockout areas.

### Q3. Station & Battery Generation Analysis
Compared battery generations, charging time, station performance, and operational challenges.

### Q4. Battery Health Analysis
Examined battery State of Health (SOH), retirement records, and average driving range across battery brands and lots.

### Q5. Pricing & Fleet Partner Analysis
Analyzed peak/off-peak pricing, partner discounts, revenue, and average energy contribution per swap.

### Q6. Rider Behavior Analysis
Studied rider inactivity, first swap experience, and low usage patterns.

### Q7. Customer Support & Budget Recommendations
Analyzed support ticket categories, resolution time, customer satisfaction, station costs, battery inventory, and partner economics.

## 🔍 Key Findings

- Three-wheelers showed higher non-completion rates than two-wheelers across the six cities analyzed.
- Peak demand was concentrated between 7 PM and 10 PM.
- Gen1 Launch batteries showed higher average charging time and non-completion rates compared to Gen3 Wave1.
- All 1,385 batteries in the analyzed Kyron 2W lots were recorded as retired.
- ZipDrop had the highest swap volume but the lowest average energy contribution per swap among the analyzed partners.
- Long queue-related support tickets had the lowest average customer satisfaction among the listed official ticket categories.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

1. Improve battery availability during peak demand hours.
2. Monitor battery health and retirement patterns.
3. Review fleet partner discount and surcharge structures.
4. Test pricing changes through controlled pilot programs.
5. Improve queue management and customer support response times.
6. Monitor rider inactivity and improve the first-swap experience.
